
========================================================================
A. Low-entropy secret in a shared prefix (templated prompt w/ a k-of-V slot)
========================================================================
Model: a system prompt whose blocks are known EXCEPT one token per block drawn
from a small set of V candidates (e.g. a field the attacker knows the format of).
  V=   10 candidates/slot, 8 blocks: mean   45.6 queries (<= 80 worst-case) -> LINEAR in V and in #blocks, NOT exponential.
  V=  100 candidates/slot, 8 blocks: mean  401.2 queries (<= 800 worst-case) -> LINEAR in V and in #blocks, NOT exponential.
  V= 1000 candidates/slot, 8 blocks: mean 4331.4 queries (<= 8000 worst-case) -> LINEAR in V and in #blocks, NOT exponential.

========================================================================
B. Why block-chaining makes it linear, not V^blocks (the crucial asymmetry)
========================================================================
A naive attacker guessing the whole prefix at once faces V^(#unknowns) space.
The hash CHAIN lets the oracle confirm each block independently once the prefix
up to it is fixed, collapsing V^n -> V*n. Demonstration at V=50:
  naive joint search space  : V^n = 50^6 = 15,625,000,000
  block-chained oracle cost : 199 queries (success=True)
  speedup                   : ~78,517,588x

========================================================================
C. High-entropy secret is safe (the defense the channel already has by accident)
========================================================================
If the unknown slot is a full high-entropy token (V ~ vocab = 32000) AND there is
more than one unknown per block, per-block cost ~ V^(unknowns/block); recovery
becomes infeasible. The channel only threatens LOW-entropy / templated secrets.
  1 unknown token(s)/block @ vocab 32000 -> ~32,000 queries/block (cheap)
  2 unknown token(s)/block @ vocab 32000 -> ~1,024,000,000 queries/block (infeasible)
  3 unknown token(s)/block @ vocab 32000 -> ~32,768,000,000,000 queries/block (infeasible)

========================================================================
D. What a defense must do (mapped to vLLM knobs)
========================================================================
cache_salt (per tenant/session): the victim's blocks hash differently from the
  attacker's identical candidates -> EVERY probe MISSES -> oracle destroyed,
  recovery cost -> infinite. This is the clean fix and exp3 prices its TTFT cost.
disable cross-request sharing / per-tenant KV pools: same effect, coarser.
rate-limiting probes: raises query cost linearly; does NOT change the V*n scaling,
  so it only slows a low-entropy-secret attack, does not stop it.
